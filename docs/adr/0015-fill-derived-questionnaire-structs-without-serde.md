# Fill derived questionnaire structs without serde

Typed decode of a derived questionnaire — materializing validated `Answers` as the application's own struct — is generated by `#[derive(Questionnaire)]` itself rather than delegated to `serde::Deserialize`. The questionnaire type universe is closed by construction (`String`, `PathBuf`, `bool`, `Option<T>`, `Vec<T>`, nested questionnaire structs, and choice enums), so the derive, which already walks every field to lower the definition, emits direct per-field construction from decoded values; a general deserialization framework buys nothing over that closed set. The decisive risk with serde was drift through a foreign attribute namespace: a struct that also derives `Serialize`/`Deserialize` for unrelated reasons may carry `#[serde(rename)]`/`#[serde(rename_all)]`, silently desynchronizing serde's field names from the question IDs the derive computed — a failure the framework could only prevent by policing another crate's attributes forever.

The consequences accepted: the derive owns the filling code for the closed type universe (a bounded amount of additional macro surface), diagnostics for fill failures stay in the framework's voice, and `serde` never enters the questionnaire contract — a questionnaire struct needs no serde derives, and one that has them for other purposes cannot have its questionnaire behavior altered by them. Because field-level decode success guarantees a value of the declared shape at every path, generated filling makes "decoded but unfillable" unrepresentable rather than a translated deserialization error. Applications converting decoded structs onward to serde-based formats do so outside the questionnaire boundary, exactly as they would any other domain type.
